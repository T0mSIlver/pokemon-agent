## Setup and the first rival
<!-- slug: setup -->
<!-- summary: Options, one-char names, Squirtle, and the Rival 1 fight. -->

Wipe any save with Up + B + Select on the title screen. In Options set text speed FAST, battle animations OFF, battle style SHIFT. Shift is temporary — it becomes SET on the way out of Pewter.

Name the player and the rival a single character each. Every extra letter is text that has to scroll on every mention for the rest of the run. Name the starter one character too.

Take Squirtle (the middle ball on Oak's table). Squirtle is not the run's damage dealer — Nidoking is — but Bubble is what beats Brock, and Squirtle stays on the team afterwards as a switch-in and a sacrifice.

Rival 1 (Bulbasaur, Lv5): Tail Whip once or twice, then Tackle until it drops. If Bulbasaur opens with Growl, Tail Whip again on turn 2. If Growl has stacked enough that Tackle is doing 1 damage and Bulbasaur still has 4+ HP, Tail Whip again rather than grinding it out.

This fight must be won. The prize money and the experience are both routed. After the win, do not mash A — mash B, or wait for a text box first. Mashing A can start a conversation with the rival before his scripted exit, which costs time.

Reset check: if the fight ran 10+ turns, or Bulbasaur fainted at 2:50 or later on the timer, look at Squirtle's Lv6 stats and consider resetting on 11 Special.

## Nidoran on Route 22
<!-- slug: nidoran -->
<!-- summary: The Route 22 catch the whole run is built on. -->

Between the two Route 1 trips, faint one wild encounter — a Lv2-3 Rattata or a Lv2 Pidgey, nothing higher. That kill is what puts Squirtle at Lv8 for the Viridian Forest Bug Catcher.

Collect Oak's Parcel from the Viridian mart, run it back to the lab, return north, and buy Poké Balls on the way through. Six to eight; eight is the default. Six only if the plan is to reset after two failed Nidoran catches, because balls are also needed later for HM mules.

Go west to Route 22 and hunt a male Nidoran, level 3 or 4. Name it one character. The level-4 catch is worth noticeably more than the level-3 catch — it moves the experience thresholds all the way to Rock Tunnel — so runners RNG-manipulate for it with a DSum step count rather than taking whatever walks out.

Throw balls straight at a Lv4 Nidoran. Tackle a Lv3 Nidoran once first to bring it into catch range. If a Lv5 Spearow appears, catch it — it is a serviceable Fly mule. Catching that Spearow on the first ball shifts the RNG, so re-seed with 5 out, 6 in, 12 out before the standard 4 in, 2 out, 6 in, 11 out cycle.

If more than half the balls are gone to Spearow, start Tackling Lv4 Nidorans once or twice so the catches stop failing.

## Viridian Forest
<!-- slug: viridian-forest -->
<!-- summary: One Bug Catcher, two pickups, and the party-order menu. -->

North out of Viridian, take the hidden Potion in the cut tree on the left, then straight through Route 2 into the forest.

Grab the Antidote on the way up. Only the red-tinted grass tiles can produce encounters, so the routed path threads between them. If Squirtle is under 10 HP, heal it and also take the hidden Potion in front of the Bug Catcher.

Bug Catcher (Weedle, Lv9): Tail Whip twice, then Tackle. Under 5 HP, heal. If Squirtle gets poisoned, do not cure it mid-fight — the Antidote goes in afterwards.

That kill puts Squirtle at Lv8, assuming the Route 1 encounter was fainted. Check its Special: 13 means heal to full before Brock; 14-15 means heal only below 20 HP.

Then, out of the grass, one menu in this exact order: swap Squirtle with Nidoran so Nidoran leads, Potion Squirtle if needed, Antidote Squirtle if needed. The order matters because the switch is what makes the heal target correct.

Exit north to Pewter City.

## Brock
<!-- slug: brock -->
<!-- summary: Squirtle switches in, Bubble twice, then the Nidoran DV check. -->

Walk into the gym and hug the left wall to slip past the gym trainer. Only Brock is fought.

Nidoran is leading, so Brock's Geodude (Lv12) comes out against it. Switch to Squirtle, Bubble two or three times. Switch back to Nidoran between Geodude and Onix so the same trick works again — Onix (Lv14) comes out, switch to Squirtle, Bubble two or three times.

If Onix uses Bide on the switch-in turn, Tail Whip once instead of attacking. If it uses Screech, hold B; the text scrolls faster.

Onix's experience levels Nidoran to Lv8, and this is the run's real decision point. Read Nidoran's Lv8 stats against the DV table: perfect is 16 Attack / 14 Defense / 15 Speed / 13 Special with 27 HP. Attack 14 (DV 0-4) is a reset. So is the exact spread 15/11/13/11. Everything else is runnable; whether to keep it depends on the timer.

Attack DV is the number that matters most. 14-15 hits three damage ranges that decide runs later: the Cerulean rival's Pidgeotto, the Machop above Nugget Bridge, and the Rock Tunnel Oddish.

Then the Pewter mart: buy 10 Potions, or 8-9 if Nidoran's Speed DV is known to be 13 or higher. On the way east out of town, open Options and switch battle style from Shift to Set.

## Route 3
<!-- slug: route-3 -->
<!-- summary: Four bug fights on a Horn Attack PP budget; catch a flier. -->

Route 3 is a PP problem, not a damage problem. Horn Attack has 15 PP and there is no way to restore it until Route 25. The counts below are the standard number of Horn Attacks — aim to use one fewer than listed by finishing with Tackle whenever Tackle will kill.

Bug Catcher 1 — Caterpie Lv10, Weedle Lv10, Caterpie Lv10: Leer, then two Horn Attacks each. The Weedle and the second Caterpie are the good Tackle-finish candidates. Heal to full or near full afterwards.

Youngster (the shorts guy) — Rattata Lv11, Ekans Lv11: Leer then two Horn Attacks. This is the most dangerous fight on the route. With 13 Defense and a turn-1 Tail Whip, Rattata two-shots Nidoran, so heal after each hit until it wastes a turn. After Leering the Ekans, reorder moves so Horn Attack sits in slot 1. Expect two heals across the first two Route 3 fights; more than four heals total will cause Potion problems later.

Bug Catcher 2 — Weedle Lv9 (2 Horn Attacks), Kakuna Lv9 (3), Caterpie Lv9 (2), Metapod Lv9 (3). String Shot from the lead Weedle is the thing that ruins it: two String Shots make Nidoran slower than the Kakuna.

Bug Catcher 3 — Caterpie Lv11 (2), Metapod Lv11 (4). With fewer than 4 Horn Attack PP entering this, spend an extra turn on the Caterpie rather than an extra Horn Attack.

Then east through the grass to dodge the Lass. If no Spearow was caught on Route 22, catch a Pidgey or Spearow here as the Fly mule. Heal at the Pokémon Center before Mt. Moon.

## Mt. Moon
<!-- slug: mt-moon -->
<!-- summary: The route's biggest branch: Water Gun, the optional Hiker, or neither. -->

Mt. Moon is where the run forks, and the fork is decided by Nidoran's Attack DV.

Three viable lines:

1. Skip Water Gun, farm 177 exp from wild encounters (138 with a Lv4 Nidoran). Gives Lv18 for the Cerulean rival's Pidgeotto, Lv23 and Thrash before the Nugget Bridge Mankey, Lv25 for Misty's Staryu, Lv30 for the Rock Tunnel Oddish.
2. Skip Water Gun and skip encounters. Everything lands one level lower: Lv17 Pidgeotto, Lv24 Staryu, Lv29 Oddish. Faster, and it produces better red-bar level-ups later, but the ranges get thin.
3. Take Water Gun and fight the optional Hiker. Same levels as line 1, plus the faster and safer Hiker on Route 25.

Rule of thumb: Attack DV 14-15 can skip encounters. DV 13 can skip, but Pidgeotto, Geodude and Oddish all become real threats. DV 11-12 and Staryu may live. DV 10 or below, do not skip — take the exp or the Hiker. A good timer favours the Hiker, because it is the safer line.

Catch a Paras on sight; it is the best HM mule available here. Weaken with Horn Attack, except that a Lv16-17 Nidorino should Tackle a Lv8 Paras, and a Lv16-18 Nidoking should Tackle a Lv10 Paras.

Fights: Super Nerd 1 (Magnemite Lv11, Voltorb Lv11) — two Horn Attacks then Poison Sting each. Then the Rare Candy east of him and the Escape Rope above. Lass (Oddish Lv11, Bellsprout Lv11) — Horn Attacks. Optional Hiker (two Geodude Lv10, Onix Lv10) — Water Gun. Rocket Grunt (Zubat Lv13, Rattata Lv13). Super Nerd 2 (Grimer, Voltorb, Koffing, all Lv12).

Take the Moon Stone in the first floor's top-left corner. When to evolve matters: Nidorino cannot reliably faint Lv10 Zubats, but Nidoking's cry costs 0.6s per encounter, so evolve just before the floor where the bigger Zubats appear. Take the Helix Fossil and leave west.

## Cerulean and the second rival
<!-- slug: cerulean-rival -->
<!-- summary: Heal, hidden Rare Candy, then Rival 2 on the bridge approach. -->

Straight through Route 4 to Cerulean. Heal at the Center, then go to the house in the far north-west of town and pick up the hidden Rare Candy in the back garden. That candy is half of the pair that buys Thrash later.

Head east and north to trigger Rival 2.

Pidgeotto (Lv18): three Horn Attacks, then Poison Sting. Pidgeotto dies to the first three Horn Attacks unless the Attack DV is poor or Mt. Moon exp was skipped. This is the fight the Mt. Moon decision was really about.

Abra (Lv15): one Horn Attack. Rattata (Lv15): Horn Attack, then Poison Sting. Bulbasaur (Lv17): two Horn Attacks, then Poison Sting.

Sand-Attack is the danger. If Pidgeotto lands one and is already in range to die to a single Horn Attack, switch in the Fly mule and let it faint rather than eat a second Sand-Attack on Nidoking. Exceptions: keep Nidoking in if the badge boost from Sand-Attack is needed to one-shot Pidgeotto, or could one-shot the Rattata, or the run is already behind.

## Nugget Bridge, Route 25 and Thrash
<!-- slug: nugget-bridge -->
<!-- summary: Six bridge fights, Rare Candies to Lv23 for Thrash, then Bill. -->

Talk to the first five bridge trainers from below so the exclamation-mark walk-up never happens.

Bug Catcher (Caterpie Lv14, Weedle Lv14): Horn Attack then Poison Sting each.

Lass 1 (Pidgey Lv14, NidoranF Lv14). Do not switch out on Sand-Attack here — Pidgey cannot punish the mules the way Pidgeotto can, and only three landed attacks are needed.

Youngster (Rattata Lv14, Ekans Lv14, Zubat Lv14). At Lv19 the Rattata is a damage range; if a heal is needed, heal on turn 1 rather than before the fight, because Rattata may spend the turn on Tail Whip and hand over an Attack boost.

Lass 2 (Pidgey Lv16, NidoranF Lv16). Jr. Trainer (Mankey Lv18) — Mankey's Karate Chop always crits and does up to 18 at Lv20, so heal beforehand if death is on the table. Rocket Grunt (Ekans Lv15, Zubat Lv15).

Somewhere in this block Nidoking hits Lv21. That is the cue: use both Rare Candies to reach Lv23 and learn Thrash. Teach it over Poison Sting (slot 4), unless an Oddish still needs catching — then delete Tackle, or Leer if Tackle is already gone. Exactly when Lv21 arrives depends on the Mt. Moon branch: Hiker strats reach it after the Youngster, encounter-exp strats after Lass 2, skip strats after the Mankey.

On Route 25, fight the top Hiker (Machop Lv15, Geodude Lv15) if Water Gun was skipped, otherwise the bottom Hiker (Onix Lv17, one Water Gun). Then Lass 1, Jr. Trainer, Lass 2, dodging everyone else. Pick up the hidden Elixer and/or Ether here — these are the only PP restores before Celadon.

Talk to Bill, say yes, then menu: Potion Nidoking toward full, then Escape Rope out.

## Misty
<!-- slug: misty -->
<!-- summary: One gym trainer, Thrash Misty, then the Rocket and Route 5-6. -->

Take the left path in Cerulean Gym to skip the first trainer. Fight the Jr. Trainer beside Misty (Goldeen Lv19, two Thrashes), then heal off Peck and Supersonic damage — the menu time is worth it.

How much HP to arrive with depends on Speed: with Speed DV 13-15, Nidoking outspeeds Starmie and about 50 HP is enough. Otherwise aim for 70+, because Starmie's Bubblebeam does 35-48 depending on Special DV, and a critical does up to 84.

Misty — Staryu (Lv18): one Thrash. Starmie (Lv21): two or three Thrashes. At Lv24 the Staryu is a range unless Attack DV is 14-15. Missing that range, or failing to two-shot Starmie, risks Thrash confusion. On confusion, almost always switch out: to Squirtle if Nidoking needs a heal turn, to the Fly mule if not. With Squirtle in and Nidoking not faster than Starmie, use Bubble and hope for the speed drop.

Afterwards, cross town to the burgled house in the north-east, walk straight out the back, and fight the Rocket there (Machop Lv17, Drowzee Lv17, both Thrash). If healing before that fight, use the same menu to teach TM11 Bubblebeam over Tackle or Water Gun.

South to Route 5, into the Underground Path. The hidden Full Restore sits left of the staircase — hold A stepping off the stairs to skip the item jingle. Out the far end to Route 6.

On Route 6, minimise grass steps if Paras or Spearow is already caught; otherwise use the Oddish DSum chart. Fight the male Jr. Trainer first (Spearow, Raticate) if HP allows, since the female's three Pidgeys can chip Nidoking to death with Quick Attack.

## Surge and the bike
<!-- slug: surge -->
<!-- summary: S.S. Anne, Rival 3, HM01 Cut, the can puzzle, then Thunderbolt. -->

In Vermilion, shop before the S.S. Anne only if both Route 25 PP items were collected and TM34 is in item slot 2. Buy 3 Super Potions and 3 Repels.

On the ship, heal to at least 23 HP before Rival 3 (15 if playing risky or short on Potions). Rival 3 — Pidgeotto Lv19 (Horn Attack, Bubblebeam), Raticate Lv16 (Thrash or two Bubblebeams, depending on HP and Defense DV), Kadabra Lv18 (Thrash), Ivysaur Lv20 (two Thrashes). Sand-Attack from Pidgeotto means switching to a mule. Confusion on Ivysaur means feeding it Squirtle or the Fly mule.

Take HM01 from the captain and leave.

Post-ship shopping: sell the Nugget (5000) and TM34 (1000), and the Antidote if it survived and the Elixer was found. Buy 3 Super Potions and 3 Repels. If the party has a Spearow but no Cut mule, trade the Spearow for Dux in the house by the tree.

Menu on the tile above the Cut tree, in order: teach Bubblebeam if not already taught, swap the Repels up the item list, teach HM01 Cut to the mule, teach TM28 Dig to Paras (or Squirtle), then Cut.

The gym's can puzzle: check bottom-left, bottom-middle, middle-left, top-middle, top-left, middle-right, top-right, bottom-right until the first switch trips; the second can is always adjacent to the first.

Surge — Voltorb (Lv21), Pikachu (Lv18), Raichu (Lv24). Lead with Thrash on Attack DV 9-15 and under 25 HP; lead with Horn Attack on DV 0-8 and finish Voltorb with Bubblebeam; lead with Bubblebeam when farming red bar off Sonicboom.

Then the Pokémon Fan Club for the Bike Voucher, Dig back to Cerulean, trade for the Bicycle, and teach TM24 Thunderbolt over Leer.

## Rock Tunnel and Celadon
<!-- slug: rock-tunnel -->
<!-- summary: Route 9 with Repels, the tunnel, then the Celadon shopping block. -->

Cut the two trees east of Cerulean and bike into Route 9. Fight the Jr. Trainer (Oddish, Bellsprout, Oddish, Bellsprout, all Lv18 — Thrash) and the Bug Catcher (Caterpie, Weedle, Venonat, all Lv20). Thunderbolt the Caterpie if Attack DV is 0-12, so a short Thrash does not leave Nidoking confused in red bar against Venonat. Bike past the rest and Repel a few steps before the entrance.

Rock Tunnel runs on three Repels — one at the entrance, one about 20 steps after the first Jr. Trainer, one about five steps before the Hiker. Fold any needed Potion into the same menu as a Repel.

Fights inside: Pokémaniac 1 (Cubone — Bubblebeam; Slowpoke — Thunderbolt), Pokémaniac 2 (Slowpoke — Thunderbolt), Jr. Trainer F 1 (Oddish, Bulbasaur — Thrash), Hiker (Geodude, Geodude, Graveler — all Bubblebeam), Jr. Trainer F 2 (Meowth, Oddish, Pidgey — Thrash).

The Rock Tunnel Oddish is the run-killer: Sleep Powder in red bar usually ends the run, and Stun Spore costs a lot of time. At Lv29 it survives Thrash roughly half the time; at Lv30 it usually does not.

South to Lavender, west onto Route 8, fight only the Gambler (Growlithe, Vulpix — lead Bubblebeam unless Attack DV exceeds Special DV by 5+), then the Underground Path to Celadon.

Celadon Mart, floor by floor: 2F buy TM07 Horn Drill and 10 Super Repels (7000); 4F buy a Poké Doll (1000); 6F buy a Fresh Water and a Soda Pop from the vending machine, hand both to the girl for TM13 Ice Beam and TM48 Rock Slide, then buy one more Fresh Water; 5F buy X items.

## Fly, the tower, and Silph Co.
<!-- slug: fly-and-silph -->
<!-- summary: HM02, Pokemon Tower, Cycling Road, Safari Zone, Silph, Koga. -->

Cut the bush by the sleeping Snorlax, go west through the checkpoint, and take HM02 from the girl in the house. Menu: Super Repel, teach TM07 Horn Drill over Horn Attack, swap X Accuracy up the list, teach HM02 to the flier, teach TM48 Rock Slide over Bubblebeam, then Fly to Lavender.

Pokémon Tower. Rival 4 (Pidgeotto, Gyarados, Growlithe, Kadabra, Ivysaur) — X Accuracy, then Horn Drill everything. Set up on Pidgeotto above 19 HP; set up on Growlithe at 10-18 HP, because it has no Quick Attack and a lower crit rate. Three Channelers with Gastly — Rock Slide, which is 81% to hit twice. Gastly's Confuse Ray and Lick both end runs; on confusion, switch a mule in and let it faint. Collect two Elixers on the way. Poké Doll the Marowak ghost. Three Rocket Grunts, mostly Thunderbolt and Thrash. Take the Poké Flute from Mr. Fuji and Fly to Celadon.

Heal at the Celadon Center to reset the Dig warp. Super Repel, bike to Snorlax, swap X Speeds up the item list, Poké Flute, run from Snorlax, and onto Cycling Road. Take the hidden Rare Candy in the grass patch; take the hidden PP Up halfway down if the strat chart calls for it.

Fuchsia: Super Repel, teach Ice Beam. Safari Zone: bike; take the Carbos in the second area on low Speed DVs; take the Gold Teeth at the end; get HM03 Surf from the house, then Dig out to Celadon.

Silph Co.: elevator to 10F, Machoke grunt, Carbos and Rare Candy and TM26 Earthquake, down to 9F and teleport to 5F. Teach HM03 to Squirtle and TM26 Earthquake over Thrash. Card Key, teleporter chain 5F to 3F to 7F. Rival 5 — X Accuracy, X Speed, then Horn Drill; the Gyarados red-bar setup saves up to 40s at roughly a 40% death rate, so usually skip it. Last grunt (Cubone, Drowzee, Marowak), then Giovanni (Nidorino, Kangaskhan, Rhyhorn, Nidoqueen) — X Accuracy then Horn Drill and Ice Beam.

Dig out, Fly to Fuchsia, and clear Koga's gym: two Jugglers on Earthquake, then Koga (Koffing, Muk, Koffing, Weezing). Stall Weezing with the Poké Flute until it Selfdestructs. Take HM04 from the warden's house.

## Endgame
<!-- slug: endgame -->
<!-- summary: Three gyms in either order, Viridian, Victory Road, Elite Four. -->

Fly to Pallet. Super Repel, dump every remaining Rare Candy into Nidoking, then Surf south to Cinnabar. In the Mansion take a Carbos on the Speed DVs that need it, teach HM04 Strength to Squirtle, pick up the Rare Candy item ball and the hidden one, use them two steps before the Secret Key, then Dig out.

Erika, Blaine and Sabrina are fought in one of two orders. Erika first means Erika, Blaine, Sabrina; Blaine first means Blaine, Sabrina, Erika. Erika (Victreebel, Tangela, Vileplume) is Ice Beam and Earthquake. Blaine (Growlithe, Ponyta, Rapidash, Arcanine) is Earthquake, or X Accuracy and Horn Drill — but Growlithe must be Earthquaked if it uses Agility. Sabrina (Kadabra, Mr. Mime, Venomoth, Alakazam) is Earthquake throughout, with an X Speed on Mr. Mime if needed.

Viridian Gym: fight the CoolTrainer (Rhyhorn — Ice Beam), then the Black Belt (Machoke, Machop, Machoke). The Black Belt line depends entirely on Attack DV — 13-15 Earthquakes all three, 11-12 uses an X Special first, 0-10 uses X Accuracy and Horn Drills. Then leave and re-enter the gym to reset trainer positions, and take the now-clear path to Giovanni (Rhyhorn, Dugtrio, Nidoqueen, Nidoking, Rhydon).

Route 22 Rival 6 (Pidgeot, Rhyhorn, Gyarados, Growlithe, Alakazam, Venusaur). Pidgeot and Growlithe only use Agility, so setting up on them is free.

Route 23 has a hidden Max Ether on the top-left square of the island past the Marsh Badge check — take it if Horn Drill PP is short (Lorelei needs 4 Drills and an Earthquake, or 5 Drills on bad Attack). Victory Road runs on precise movement between Super Repels, with Strength on the final boulder.

Indigo Plateau: deposit HM mules first — usually Squirtle and Paras/Oddish, keeping the flier for the Lorelei switch. Do not take the Center. Lorelei is switch, then X Accuracy, then Horn Drill. Bruno is X Accuracy then Ice Beam and Horn Drills. Agatha is the highest-variance fight: X Speed turn 1, then a decision table on Gengar's move and current HP. Lance is X Special and Thunderbolt on Gyarados, then Ice Beam everything, arriving at an HP where Hydro Pump drops into red bar. The Champion is X Accuracy, X Speed, then Horn Drill.
