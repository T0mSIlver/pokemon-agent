## Pallet Town and the first steps
<!-- slug: start -->
<!-- summary: Starter choice, Rival 1, Oak's Parcel, and the Route 1 loop. -->

Leave the house, try to walk north out of town, and Oak drags the player back to the lab. Three balls on the table: Bulbasaur (left), Charmander (right), Squirtle (middle).

For a normal playthrough, Bulbasaur is the easy mode — it beats the first two gyms outright with Vine Whip. Squirtle is the balanced pick and handles Brock and Blaine. Charmander is the hard start: it is at a disadvantage against both Brock and Misty and needs help until it learns Ember at Lv9 and evolves.

Rival 1 happens immediately, at Lv5, with whichever starter beats yours. Lower its Attack or Defense once with the stat move, then attack until it drops. Losing is survivable, but the prize money is worth having.

North through Route 1 to Viridian. Catch a Pidgey or a Rattata in the grass on the way — a second body matters as soon as the starter takes a bad hit. The Poké Mart clerk hands over Oak's Parcel; carry it back to Pallet, give it to Oak, and receive the Pokédex and five Poké Balls.

Back in Viridian, buy Poké Balls (about 10) and Potions (about 5) with the starting money. Two things in Viridian are locked for now: the gym (it is the eighth badge) and the fat man blocking Route 2 north, who moves once the Parcel is delivered.

## Viridian Forest and Route 2
<!-- slug: viridian-forest -->
<!-- summary: The first real grass; catch a Pikachu here or skip it. -->

Route 2 north is a short strip of grass with a Poké Ball on it, then the forest gate.

Viridian Forest is a maze of tall grass with several Bug Catchers scattered through it. The encounters are Caterpie, Metapod, Weedle, Kakuna and Pidgey, plus a rare Pikachu — in Red the Pikachu is around a 5% encounter. It is worth a few minutes of searching if the plan is to have an Electric type before Misty, and it is the only Pikachu in the wild in this half of the game.

Items on the floor: an Antidote, a Potion, and a Poké Ball. There is also a hidden Potion near the exit.

The Bug Catchers here are the intended level curve — fight all of them. Their Weedle and Caterpie are Lv6-9 and each fight is a few hundred experience. A starter that clears the forest fights is usually Lv10-12 leaving the north exit, which is enough for Brock without any grinding.

Caterpie and Weedle evolve fast and Butterfree learns Confusion, which is a genuinely useful move for the next three gyms. Catching one early is not a wasted slot.

Exit north to Pewter City.

## Brock and Route 3
<!-- slug: brock -->
<!-- summary: The Boulder Badge, then the long trainer gauntlet east of Pewter. -->

Pewter Gym has one trainer (a Jr. Trainer with a Diglett and a Sandshrew) and Brock. Brock has Geodude Lv12 and Onix Lv14, both Rock/Ground.

Counters, in order of how easy they are: any Water or Grass move halves their HP in one hit — Bulbasaur's Vine Whip and Squirtle's Bubble both roll over Onix. With Charmander, this is the wall: Ember does nothing, so bring a Mankey caught on Route 22 (Low Kick is super effective) or a Butterfree with Confusion, or grind to Lv12+ and use Metal Claw-less brute force with Scratch while Potions absorb Onix's Bide.

Bide is the thing to watch. Onix stores two turns of damage and returns double. If Onix starts Bide, stop attacking or switch — feeding it a big hit is how the fight is lost.

The Boulder Badge makes traded Pokémon up to Lv20 obey and enables Flash outside battle.

Also in Pewter: the museum (pay to see the Old Amber, which is only usable much later), and a man who gives away a Nugget for hearing him out on the way east.

Route 3 is a straight line east with five or six trainers on it, all Bug Catchers, Youngsters and a Lass with a Clefairy. Fight them; the money and levels are both needed. Wild Spearow, Mankey, Jigglypuff and Nidoran appear here. The Pokémon Center at the far end sits right in front of Mt. Moon and has a man selling a Magikarp for 500 — a bad deal in the short term and a Gyarados in the long term.

## Mt. Moon
<!-- slug: mt-moon -->
<!-- summary: Three floors, Team Rocket's first appearance, and the fossil choice. -->

Mt. Moon is the first dungeon. Zubat and Geodude are everywhere; Paras, Clefairy and Sandshrew also appear. Bring Repels and about 10 Poké Balls.

Trainers inside: two Super Nerds, a Lass, a Hiker, and several Rocket Grunts. The Rockets are the game introducing the plot — they are here for the fossils.

Items worth taking: a Rare Candy, an Escape Rope, TM12 Water Gun (from a Super Nerd on the first floor), a Moon Stone in the north-west corner, and several Potions. The Moon Stone evolves Clefairy, Jigglypuff, Nidorina and Nidorino — worth saving unless a Nidoking is planned, in which case use it as soon as Nidorino has the moves it needs (evolving stops level-up learnsets in Gen 1, so evolve late, not early).

At the end, a Super Nerd guards two fossils and only one can be taken:
- Helix Fossil becomes Omanyte (Water/Rock).
- Dome Fossil becomes Kabuto (Water/Rock).

Neither can be revived until the Cinnabar lab much later, and neither is necessary. Omastar has better Special; Kabutops has better Attack.

Exit west onto Route 4, which is a short ledge run down to Cerulean. There is a hidden Rare Candy and a wild-grass patch with Ekans/Sandshrew, plus the back entrance to Cerulean Cave that stays locked until the Champion is beaten.

## Misty, Nugget Bridge and Bill
<!-- slug: misty -->
<!-- summary: Cascade Badge, the bridge gauntlet, S.S. Ticket from Bill. -->

Cerulean City has the second gym, a house being robbed (the Rocket inside leaves a TM), and a bike shop selling a bicycle nobody can afford yet.

Rival 2 waits at the north end of town before Nugget Bridge: Pidgeotto Lv18, Abra Lv15, Rattata Lv15, and his starter at Lv17. He is a real step up — heal first.

Misty has Staryu Lv18 and Starmie Lv21, both pure Water. Starmie's Bubblebeam hurts. Counters: Electric (a Pikachu from Viridian Forest, or a Magnemite from the Route 10 power plant area later), or Grass (Bulbasaur's Vine Whip, Oddish, Bellsprout). Without either, level up and out-trade with a Nidoking or a Butterfree using Confusion — but expect to burn Potions. The Cascade Badge enables Cut outside battle and makes traded Pokémon to Lv30 obey.

Nugget Bridge is five trainers in a row on Route 24. Beat all five without healing between them and the Rocket at the top hands over a Nugget (worth 5000 at any Mart) — then attacks. There is no way to heal mid-bridge, so arrive at full HP with a deep bench.

Route 25 continues east past several Hikers, Lasses and Jr. Trainers to Bill's house. Bill has turned himself into a Pokémon; talk to the "Pokémon" in his lab, agree to help, use the machine in the back, and talk to him again for the S.S. Ticket.

Along the way: TM19 Seismic Toss lies on Route 25, and there is a hidden Ether above the Hiker. On the way back, the robbed house in Cerulean is now open — the Rocket in the garden drops TM28 Dig.

## Vermilion, the S.S. Anne and Surge
<!-- slug: vermilion-surge -->
<!-- summary: HM01 Cut, the S.S. Anne, and Surge's trash-can switches. -->

South from Cerulean on Route 5, through the Underground Path (Route 5 to Route 6 entrance), and out onto Route 6 into Vermilion City.

Vermilion contains: the S.S. Anne dock, the Pokémon Fan Club, the third gym, and a trade for a Mr. Mime.

Board the S.S. Anne with the S.S. Ticket. Every cabin has a trainer in it — Gentlemen, Sailors, Fishermen, a Super Nerd — and the ship is the best money in the early game. Trash-can rummaging in the kitchen yields items. Halfway up, Rival 3 blocks the stairs: Pidgeotto Lv19, Raticate Lv16, Kadabra Lv18, starter Lv20. Beat him, then talk to the seasick captain and rub his back for HM01 Cut. The ship sails away once the player disembarks, so finish everything on board first.

Cut is required to reach Surge: a small tree blocks the gym door.

Surge's gym is a puzzle. Two switches are hidden in the trash cans; the first can is arbitrary, and the second is always in one of the two cans directly adjacent to the first. Getting the second wrong resets both. Two gym trainers are in the room and can be avoided.

Lt. Surge: Voltorb Lv21, Pikachu Lv18, Raichu Lv24, all pure Electric. Ground moves are immune to everything he has — a Diglett or Dugtrio from Diglett's Cave (Route 2/Route 11) or a Sandshrew or Nidoking with Dig walks through the fight untouched. Raichu's Thunderbolt otherwise two-shots most things at this level.

The Thunder Badge enables Fly outside battle and makes traded Pokémon to Lv40 obey. Then take the Bike Voucher from the Pokémon Fan Club chairman and swap it for the bicycle in Cerulean.

## Rock Tunnel and Lavender Town
<!-- slug: rock-tunnel -->
<!-- summary: Flash, a pitch-dark cave, and the tower that needs the Silph Scope. -->

Head back to Cerulean and Cut the trees east onto Route 9, or take Route 11 and Diglett's Cave to shortcut around. Route 9 and Route 10 are dense with trainers and lead to Rock Tunnel.

Rock Tunnel is completely dark without HM05 Flash, which comes from Oak's Aide on Route 2 once 10 species are registered in the Pokédex. Flash needs the Boulder Badge to use outside battle. Going in without it is possible but miserable.

The tunnel has around six trainers (Pokémaniacs, Hikers, Jr. Trainers) and wild Zubat, Geodude, Machop and Onix. Bring Repels and Potions; there is no Center inside.

The power plant sits off Route 10 across the water and needs Surf, so it comes later. It holds Zapdos, Electabuzz, Voltorb and Magnemite.

South out of Rock Tunnel to Lavender Town: a Pokémon Center, a Name Rater, the volunteer house, and Pokémon Tower.

Pokémon Tower cannot be cleared yet. The ghost Marowak on 6F is unidentifiable and unbeatable without the Silph Scope, which is held by Team Rocket in Celadon. Fighting Channelers on the lower floors is possible but pointless until then. Note that the Ghost-type Gastly and Haunter here are immune to Normal and Fighting moves.

So: leave Lavender west along Route 8, through the Underground Path (Route 7 to Route 8), and into Celadon.

## Celadon: Erika, the department store and the Rocket Hideout
<!-- slug: celadon -->
<!-- summary: Rainbow Badge, TM shopping, and the Silph Scope from Giovanni's hideout. -->

Celadon City is the game's shopping hub and the point where a normal team stops being underpowered.

The department store, floor by floor: 2F general items and TMs including TM07 Horn Drill, TM09, TM17, TM18; 3F sells TM11 Bubblebeam and the Pokédex-adjacent items; 4F evolution stones (Fire, Water, Thunder, Leaf — 2100 each); 5F X items, vitamins (HP Up, Protein, Iron, Carbos, Calcium) and TM32; 6F vending machines and a rooftop girl who trades a Fresh Water for TM13 Ice Beam, a Soda Pop for TM48 Rock Slide, and a Lemonade for TM49.

Erika's gym is behind a row of small trees and needs Cut. Erika: Victreebel Lv29, Tangela Lv24, Vileplume Lv29, all Grass (Victreebel and Vileplume are also Poison). Fire, Ice, Flying and Psychic all hit hard; a Charizard, a Butterfree with Confusion, or Ice Beam on anything clears the room. Watch for Sleep Powder and Stun Spore — bring Awakenings or a Poké Flute-equivalent. The Rainbow Badge enables Strength outside battle and holds traded Pokémon to Lv50.

The Rocket Game Corner in Celadon hides the Rocket Hideout. Talk to the Rocket standing by the poster in the back-left, beat him, and press the switch behind the poster to open the stairs. Four basement floors of grunts, spinning-tile floors that fling the player around, and Giovanni at the bottom (Onix Lv25, Rhyhorn Lv24, Kangaskhan Lv29). Beat him for the Silph Scope. Items down there include a Moon Stone, TM07, and Rare Candies.

## Pokémon Tower, Saffron and Sabrina
<!-- slug: silph-and-sabrina -->
<!-- summary: Poke Flute, Silph Co., the Master Ball, and the Marsh Badge. -->

Return to Lavender with the Silph Scope. Climb Pokémon Tower: Channelers on every floor with Gastly, Haunter and Cubone. On 6F Rival 4 waits (Pidgeotto, Gyarados, Growlithe/Exeggcute, Kadabra, starter — all around Lv25). On the top the ghost is now identifiable as a Lv30 Marowak; beat it, then the three Rocket Grunts, and Mr. Fuji gives the Poké Flute.

The Poké Flute wakes both Snorlax — one on Route 12 south of Lavender, one on Route 16 west of Celadon. Each is a Lv30 catchable Snorlax and each blocks a road.

Saffron City is sealed by guards who want a drink. Buy any beverage from the Celadon vending machines and hand it to the guard on any Saffron gate. Inside, Team Rocket has taken the city and Silph Co.

Silph Co. is eleven floors of grunts, Scientists, teleporter pads and locked doors. The Card Key is on 5F; it opens the shutter doors that block the route upward. On 7F Rival 5 appears (Pidgeot, Gyarados, Growlithe/Exeggcute, Alakazam, starter — around Lv35). On 11F Giovanni fights again (Nidorino Lv37, Kangaskhan Lv35, Rhyhorn Lv37, Nidoqueen Lv41). Beating him frees the building, and the Silph president hands over the Master Ball — save it for a legendary or for Mewtwo.

Also inside: TM26 Earthquake, a Lapras from a scientist on 7F (a genuinely strong Water/Ice type, worth taking), Rare Candies and Full Heals.

Sabrina's gym is a grid of teleport tiles; each pad sends the player to a fixed other pad, and the route through is a specific sequence. Sabrina: Kadabra Lv38, Mr. Mime Lv37, Venomoth Lv38, Alakazam Lv43. Psychic is close to unanswerable in Gen 1 — Bug moves are the only real weakness and the only decent one is Pinsir's or Parasect's. The practical answer is a fast strong attacker that out-damages her, or Persian/Snorlax with high-power Normal moves. The Marsh Badge holds traded Pokémon to Lv70.

## Fuchsia, Koga and the Safari Zone
<!-- slug: fuchsia-koga -->
<!-- summary: Cycling Road, HM03 Surf and HM04 Strength, then the Soul Badge. -->

From Celadon, Route 16 west needs the Poké Flute for the Snorlax, then Cycling Road (Route 17) runs south — the bicycle is mandatory here. Cycling Road is a long downhill with Bikers and Cue Balls on it; the ledges mean it is one-way fast. Route 18 leads east into Fuchsia City. The alternative approach is Route 12/13/14/15 south from Lavender, which needs the other Snorlax moved and a Fishing Rod for the water sections.

Fuchsia City holds the fifth gym, the Safari Zone, and the warden's house.

The Safari Zone is a 500-entry, 30-ball, 500-step minigame where Pokémon cannot be fought, only baited, rocked and thrown at. Two things inside are required progress, not optional: HM03 Surf, from the Secret House in the deepest area, and the Gold Teeth, a floor item that the warden trades for HM04 Strength. Also inside: Chansey, Scyther, Pinsir, Kangaskhan, Tauros, Dratini (from the fishing pond), and several vitamins and TMs.

Surf and Strength unlock most of the remaining map — Strength moves the Victory Road boulders, Surf reaches Cinnabar, the Seafoam Islands and the power plant.

Koga: Koffing Lv37, Muk Lv39, Koffing Lv37, Weezing Lv43, all Poison. Psychic moves are the clean answer and Alakazam or a Psychic TM ends the fight quickly; Ground moves also work. Watch Selfdestruct from the Koffing line and Toxic from Koga's Muk. His gym is an invisible-wall maze — the visible path is not the real path, so expect to walk into walls.

The Soul Badge raises Defense and enables Surf outside battle.

## Cinnabar, Blaine and Viridian Gym
<!-- slug: cinnabar-blaine -->
<!-- summary: Surf south, the Mansion and Secret Key, then Giovanni's last gym. -->

Surf south from Pallet Town along Route 21, or east from Fuchsia along Routes 19 and 20, to reach Cinnabar Island. Route 20 also passes the Seafoam Islands, an optional two-part boulder puzzle holding Articuno.

Cinnabar has a Pokémon Lab (revives the Helix or Dome Fossil and the Old Amber into Omanyte, Kabuto or Aerodactyl, and holds three in-game trades), the Pokémon Mansion, and Blaine's gym — locked, and needing the Secret Key.

Pokémon Mansion is four floors of Scientists and Burglars, warp-panel floors and switch-activated statues that open and close paths. Wild Rattata, Grimer, Koffing, Ditto and Magmar appear. The Secret Key is on the top-right of the basement level. Items along the way include TM14 Blizzard, TM22 Solarbeam, several Rare Candies, and the journal entries about Mew.

Blaine: Growlithe Lv42, Ponyta Lv40, Rapidash Lv42, Arcanine Lv47, all Fire. Water, Rock and Ground moves all hit for double; a Blastoise, a Lapras with Surf, or Golem with Earthquake handles it. His gym is a quiz — answer correctly and the door opens; answer wrong and a trainer fights. Answering everything correctly is faster than fighting through.

The Volcano Badge raises Special.

Viridian Gym finally opens. The gym leader is Giovanni for the third time: Rhyhorn Lv45, Dugtrio Lv42, Nidoqueen Lv44, Nidoking Lv45, Rhydon Lv50 — Ground with Rock and Poison mixed in. Water, Grass and Ice all hit hard; Surf and Ice Beam are the reliable pair. The gym floor is another spin-tile puzzle and Strength is needed for the boulders.

The Earth Badge makes every traded Pokémon obey. After the win, the old man in the Viridian house is gone and Rival 6 waits on Route 22.

## Victory Road and the Elite Four
<!-- slug: elite-four -->
<!-- summary: The final gauntlet: five back-to-back fights with no healing between. -->

West out of Viridian to Route 22 for Rival 6 (Pidgeot, Rhyhorn, Gyarados, Growlithe/Exeggcute, Alakazam, starter — around Lv47). Then north through Route 23, where a guard checks each badge in turn at a series of checkpoints. All eight are needed.

Victory Road is three floors of Strength boulders pushed onto floor switches to open doors, plus a Moltres on the top floor, Rare Candies, TM17 Submission and several Full Heals. Wild Onix, Machoke, Golbat, Graveler and Marowak. Bring plenty of Repels.

Indigo Plateau has the last Poké Mart — this is the place to buy Full Restores, Revives, Max Repels and Elixirs, because once the Elite Four starts there is no shop and no Center until it ends. Twenty Full Restores and a handful of Revives is not excessive. A team around Lv50-55 is the comfortable range.

The five fights run back to back. Healing items work, but fainted Pokémon stay fainted between rooms unless revived.

- Lorelei — Dewgong Lv54, Cloyster Lv53, Slowbro Lv54, Jynx Lv56, Lapras Lv56. Water/Ice. Electric and Grass hit the Water types; Fighting and Rock hit the Ice. Cloyster's Clamp and Lapras's Blizzard are the dangerous moves.
- Bruno — Onix Lv53, Hitmonchan Lv55, Hitmonlee Lv55, Onix Lv56, Machamp Lv58. Fighting and Rock. Psychic sweeps this entire room; Water and Grass drop both Onix in one hit.
- Agatha — Gengar Lv56, Golbat Lv56, Haunter Lv55, Arbok Lv58, Gengar Lv60. Ghost/Poison. In Gen 1 Psychic is not resisted by Ghost, so Psychic works here too. Her real weapons are status: Hypnosis, Confuse Ray and Toxic.
- Lance — Gyarados Lv58, Dragonair Lv56, Dragonair Lv56, Aerodactyl Lv60, Dragonite Lv62. Ice moves hit every one of them; Blizzard or Ice Beam is the answer. Electric handles Gyarados and Aerodactyl.
- Champion (the rival) — Pidgeot Lv61, Alakazam Lv59, Rhydon Lv61, and a rotating trio built around whichever starter counters yours, ending at Lv63. This is a full six-Pokémon fight with no type theme; win it on levels and coverage.
